1\. Overall Aesthetic Vision

The art and audio direction of Ultima VII: Project Virtue must feel exactly like the 1992 original — warm, hand-crafted, and full of life — while using modern technology only to enhance clarity, lighting, and immersion.Core Mantra:  
“Pixel-art soul with modern polish.”  
We do not replace the retro isometric look with 3D models, realistic textures, or action-game visuals. We enhance the original 1992 aesthetic so it feels timeless on 4K/8K displays.This bible ensures every asset (official or UGC-created) maintains visual and auditory consistency so the world always feels like classic Ultima VII.

---

2\. Visual Style Guidelines

2.1 Perspective & Camera

* Locked isometric (top-down 45° angle) — exactly as in the original.  
* No first-person, no over-the-shoulder, no platformer-style camera.  
* Optional “legacy zoom” toggle for purists.

2.2 Pixel Art Style

* Base resolution: 320×200 upscaled to modern displays using crisp pixel-perfect scaling \+ optional light anti-aliasing.  
* Color palette: Strictly limited to the original Ultima VII 256-color palette (with approved expansion colors for new regions).  
* All assets hand-crafted or procedurally generated then hand-touched to maintain the painted, miniature-diorama feel.

2.3 Lighting & Effects

* Dynamic real-time lighting (torches, fires, spells, day/night cycle) using modern shaders.  
* Soft shadows and subtle bloom allowed only to enhance readability — never to create realistic graphics.  
* Particle effects (fire, smoke, magic) must use pixel-art style sprites.

2.4 UI Philosophy

* “The entire screen is the game world” — no permanent HUD in normal gameplay.  
* Contextual pop-ups (inventory, dialogue, spell book) pause the simulation exactly as in 1992\.  
* Clean, parchment-style fonts and borders that match the original manual aesthetic.

---

3\. Character & NPC Design Rules

* Proportions: Classic chunky pixel style (large heads, expressive faces).  
* Animation: 8–12 frames per action (walk, attack, idle, use object). All animations must feel weighty and physical.  
* Customization (Avatar): Limited to clothing dyes and simple armor pieces that fit the medieval fantasy look — no wild fantasy hair or modern outfits.  
* NPC Variety: Base templates \+ procedural clothing/hair variations within the same style guide.  
* Party Companions: Erevan, Shamino, Dupre, etc., must match their exact 1992 sprite silhouettes and color schemes.

---

4\. Environment & Object Design Rules

* Tilesets: Original Highmere/Stonereach/etc. tiles as base; new regions use expanded but compatible palettes.  
* Objects: Every interactive item must have unique sprites for multiple states (closed/open, lit/unlit, broken/intact).  
* Destruction & Physics: Damaged versions of objects must be pre-authored or procedurally dirtied while staying in style.  
* Environmental Storytelling: Clutter, bloodstains, glowing runes, and small details must tell stories at a glance.

---

5\. Audio Style Bible

5.1 Music

* Style: Orchestral with medieval instruments (lute, flute, harp, light percussion).  
* Dynamic system: Music shifts based on time of day, combat, Virtue reputation, and location.  
* Reference: Original Ultima VII soundtrack by “The Fat Man” (Marc Schaefgen) and Ken Allen — warm, melodic, slightly melancholic.  
* New tracks must feel like they could have been composed in 1992\.

5.2 Sound Effects

* All SFX must feel physical and “chunky” (wood creaking, metal clanging, fire crackling).  
* Contextual variation: Same action sounds different based on surface (sword on stone vs. sword on wood).  
* Volume and pitch variation for every object interaction to reinforce simulation depth.

5.3 Voice & Dialogue

* Key story NPCs: Full voice acting with British-accented, period-appropriate delivery.  
* Background NPCs: Text-only (to preserve performance and charm).  
* Avatar: Silent protagonist tradition maintained — player types or chooses responses.

---

6\. Technical & Production Constraints

* All assets must be compatible with the simulation system (Document \#4) and UGC tools (Document \#7).  
* File size and memory budgets defined so UGC creators cannot break performance.  
* Style validation tool built into the editor (auto-flags assets that deviate from palette or proportions).

7\. Prototype Scope – “Highmere Visual & Audio Vertical Slice”

Must be complete in first playable build:

* Full Highmere town with every object using final art style \+ dynamic lighting.  
* Original Ultima VII soundtrack loop \+ 20+ contextual SFX.  
* 8 key NPCs with full animations and voice lines for opening dialogue.  
* UI mockups and parchment-style fonts implemented.  
* One procedural dungeon using the exact same art rules as the hand-crafted town.

Success Metric:  
A player should look at the screen and immediately say, “This is Ultima VII — only clearer and more alive.”
